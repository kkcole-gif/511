Setup

Two Groups, Running at the Same Time

~5 min

Rather than watching fourteen presentations in sequence, we'll split into two groups and run simultaneously — each group sees seven five-minute lessons, then we come back together to compare what we observed.

Group A Stays in this room

Seven presenters. Rotate in the order on the sign-up sheet. One person keeps time — raise a hand at 4:30 to signal the presenter.

Group B Moves to the hall or second space

Same structure: seven presenters in rotation, one timekeeper. Reconvene in this room at the 35-minute mark.

Timekeeper role: Each group needs one volunteer to track time. At 4:30, give a visual signal. At 5:00, say "time." The presenter wraps in the next 30 seconds.

01

Presentations

35 min, parallel groups

Five minutes each. While you wait to present, you're an observer. Use the three questions below to structure what you notice — you'll need your observations for the reflection.

1 Genre
What genre does this lesson plan perform? What does its structure assume about how learning works?
2 Social Action
What is this lesson actually doing for students? What social action does it enact — what situation does it respond to?
3 Feedback
One concrete strength you saw. One question for the presenter to take into revision — specific to their lesson, not generic.

02

Written Reflection

~8 min

Both groups reconvene here. Take eight minutes to write before we discuss.

Write in response to this Name one pattern you saw across the presentations in your group. What did multiple lessons have in common — in structure, in what they assumed, in what they asked students to do?
