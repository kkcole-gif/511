Commitments

Writing studies holds no single uncontested doctrine. Its foundational claims are a contested but durable set of commitments about writing, learning, language, rhetoric, labor, and institutional conditions. Each one appears here with the first line of its limit, its register, and the week testing it. Each page gives the limit in full and the evidence, from inside the field and from outside it.

The wording comes from Cole (2026), Field Commitments in Writing Studies: A Claims-Based Map. Keep it open all semester.

The twelve commitments

Commitment Its limit Register Week
1. Writing is rhetorical and situated Situating writing should not mean teaching accommodation to dominant institutional norms alone. Pedagogy 2
2. Writing is a social practice, not merely an individual cognitive skill “Discourse community” is easily pictured as more coherent, more stable, and more equally accessible than any community is. Activity 3
3. Writing is a mode of learning and inquiry Writing does not automatically produce learning. Activity 3
4. Writing is recursive, developmental, and processual “Process” does not reduce to a uniform sequence. Activity 4
5. Revision is re-seeing, not merely proofreading Not every assignment merits multiple drafts. Activity 7
6. Writing develops through use in consequential contexts Authenticity is not automatic, and simulated genres retain value. Method 5
7. Disciplines are ways of knowing, doing, and writing The claim should not reify disciplines, which contain competing methods, genres, ideologies, and publics and which change over time. Method 5
8. Writing development is lifelong and context-dependent The corrective to the one-course solution should not become an unfunded mandate for all faculty. Method 11
9. Transfer is possible but not automatic Transfer should not be framed as a student deficit. Method 11
10. Reading, research, writing, and citation are intertwined literacy practices Citation regimes also enact exclusion, Eurocentrism, gatekeeping, and uneven access to scholarly publication. Activity and method 10
11. Language difference is a resource, not proof of deficiency The commitment requires more than welcoming language. Pedagogy 6
12. Writing and communication are multimodal and technologically mediated The commitment should not be technologically deterministic, and multimodal work is not inherently engaging or equitable. Activity and pedagogy 13 and 15

The conditions instruction requires

Condition Its limit Register Week
Assessment is local, multiple, and purpose-driven The alternative is not the absence of standards. Pedagogy and method 7
Class size is a pedagogical and labor condition These figures are norms and policy recommendations grounded in workload analysis and professional standards. Method 8
Writing belongs throughout an education: WAC, WID, and CAC Faculty development does not resolve structural problems. Pedagogy, at the scale of a program 12

Tensions to hold

The map names six tensions and treats each as generative. Each one sits in the week where it presses hardest.

  • Individual writer development and social and institutional conditions. Week 3
  • Process and product. Week 4
  • General writing knowledge and disciplinary specificity. Week 5
  • Support and standardization. Week 6
  • Writing for learning and writing for assessment. Week 7
  • Efficiency and educational quality. Week 8
