Workshop Teams

Sign up in Week 1 for one of five Workshop Teaching Teams of three or four. Your team holds two jobs all semester.

Peer response is one of the six practices the field holds, and the map states its caution plainly: it is not self-executing. It requires models, shared purposes, criteria, time, accountability, and attention to power. Your Workshop Team is built to supply those.

Job one: lead one Thursday and write one Program Memo

Your team designs and leads the Thursday workshop in Week 4, 5, 8, 10, or 12, built around the week's commitment, so the workshop enacts the claim it teaches. Each team week has a suggested activity on this site, and your team is free to adapt it. Your team also writes the Program Memo for its week.

Sign up in Week 1. The sign-up link arrives in Moodle in Week 1.

Job two: read each other's work at every checkpoint

Your team reads each member's individual work before I do. Each occasion is a practice colleagues use on each other's teaching.

  • Week 3: a writing group. You trade drafts of the Literacy Narrative and the Teaching Philosophy.
  • Week 6: prompt review. You read each other's assignment prompt as a first-year student would.
  • Week 7: norming. You annotate the same sample papers and compare what each of you called strong, weak, or unclear.
  • Week 8: lesson review, in class. You read each other's lesson against the prompt it supports.
  • Week 13: rubric review. You test each other's rubric on a sample paper.
  • Week 16: the roundtable. You leave your team and sit with colleagues who have not read your work.

The protocol

A reader does five things, in order.

  1. State what you understood the piece's purpose and audience to be.
  2. Name the strongest move already in the draft.
  3. Name one place where you needed more as a reader.
  4. Offer one suggestion tied to a stated criterion.
  5. Leave the decision with the writer.

How each exchange runs

You post a complete draft in its Portfolio Doc tab by the posting date. Your teammates respond as comments in the tab by the response date. You revise before the next checkpoint, and the earlier version stays visible in the tab.

What I read, and what the labor expectation is

I confirm each exchange happened and the labor expectation was met. It is met when three things are true: your complete draft reached your team by the posting date, you responded to each teammate's draft using the protocol, and your final version is in its tab by its Sunday deadline.

Your team responds at every checkpoint, and I write no comments there. I read closely and respond in writing twice: to the complete Assignment Sequence in Week 15 and to the finished Portfolio Doc in Week 16.
