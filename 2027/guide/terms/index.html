Defined once

Terms

Every term and abbreviation the course uses.

Register
One of the three ways this course reads the word “writing”: as activity, as pedagogy, or as method.
Commitments
The twelve foundational claims the field holds about writing, introduced in Week 1.
Conditions
The three conditions instruction requires: local assessment, class size and labor, and writing throughout an education.
Kinds of evidence
Causal (an experiment, or a meta-analysis of experiments, tests an effect), observational (a study counts or measures what exists and assigns nothing), qualitative (a study interprets cases, texts, or interviews), and normative (a position argues what ought to hold).
Portfolio Doc
Your single Google Doc with four tabs: Introduction, Literacy Narrative, Assignment Sequence, and Pedagogy Statement. “Portfolio” always means this document.
Workshop Team
Your team of three or four. It leads one Thursday, writes one Program Memo, and reads each member's work at every checkpoint.
Program Memo
The one-to-two-page memo your team addresses to a named stakeholder in the week it leads.
Checkpoint
One of the three points where your Workshop Team reads your Assignment Sequence: the prompt, the lesson, and the rubric with its feedback plan.
The protocol
The five steps a reader follows when responding to a teammate's draft.
Commitments Close
The short note ending most assignments. It names the commitments the piece enacts or complicates.
Reflection Prompt
The question on each week's page. It guides the reflection in your Perusall annotation and opens Tuesday's discussion.
Norming
Comparing responses to the same student texts until readers' standards converge.
ENG 101
NC State's first-year writing course.
CCCC
Conference on College Composition and Communication, the field's main professional organization.
15/20/60
CCCC's class-size standard: 15 students preferred per writing section, 20 maximum, and no more than 60 writing students per instructor per term.
UDL
Universal Design for Learning.
WPA
Writing program administrator.
WAC, WID, CAC
Writing across the curriculum, writing in the disciplines, communication across the curriculum.
