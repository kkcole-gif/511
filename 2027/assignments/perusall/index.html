You annotate each day's readings in Perusall before the class where we discuss them. Annotation is low-stakes writing, the first of the six practices, and it is the course's standing test of Commitment 3: writing is a mode of learning and inquiry.

What you produce, for each reading

  • 3 to 6 passages, each with a comment and a page number. Choose passages you find compelling or problematic.
  • One brief summary of the key points, in your own words.
  • 2 or 3 questions about the reading.
  • One short reflection connecting the reading to your interests, your teaching, or your experience. The week's Reflection Prompt guides it.

When

  • Tuesday's readings before Tuesday's class. Thursday's readings before Thursday's class.
  • Weeks 3, 6, 13, and 15, when an assignment or a checkpoint is due: choose one of the week's readings for full annotation and draw on the others in your own work.
  • Week 6: no class meets on Tuesday, September 28. Tuesday's readings are due in Perusall on the usual schedule, and discussion continues in the threads.
  • Weeks 9 and 14: no readings and no annotation.

How it counts

Annotations count toward the labor record when they are complete and on time. They carry no grade. The map's caution on low-stakes writing sets the terms: the writing must stay low stakes.
