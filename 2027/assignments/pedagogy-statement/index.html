The final project is what you have already built. Revisit every tab. Polish each one without deleting your earlier writing. The distance between September and December is part of what you present.

What you produce

  • Composition Pedagogy Statement (1000–1500 words, Pedagogy Statement tab): A statement of teaching you would send to a search committee, revised from your Week 3 Teaching Philosophy Draft. Say which commitments you hold, which you contest, and which remain unsettled. Say where AI sits in your teaching, drawing on the Week 15 policy lab. Keep the Week 3 draft visible below the statement under a clear header.
  • Sources, tagged (end of the Pedagogy Statement tab): List the five or six sources from the semester your statement rests on. Give each a full citation and three tags: the commitment it bears on, the register it works in, and the kind of evidence it offers (causal, observational, qualitative, or normative). Then check your list against the course's pattern in one or two sentences. Do your activity-register sources carry the causal evidence?
  • Literacy Narrative and Assignment Sequence, revisited: Bring each to a form you would use. Revise the sequence where my response or the roundtable gives you reason to.
  • Portfolio Introduction (300–500 words, written last, Introduction tab): Address an outside reader: a search committee, a new WPA, a future chair. Say how the portfolio is organized and which commitments organize it.
  • Portfolio Roundtable (Tuesday, December 7): You sit at a table of three or four colleagues from other Workshop Teams. Each of you gets 15 minutes: five to present your Portfolio Introduction and one artifact, ten for the table's response, using the protocol. Your table has not read your work, so they are the first outside readers your Introduction is written for. The last 15 minutes return to the whole room, where each table reports one pattern it saw.
