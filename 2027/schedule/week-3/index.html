The commitments this week tests

Commitment 2 · Activity register

Writing is a social practice, not merely an individual cognitive skill

What it means for teaching. Writing is learned through participation in communities: feedback, reading, collaboration, uptake, and institutional practice. Instruction attends to discourse communities, genres, activity systems, and relations of power.

Its limit. “Discourse community” is easily pictured as more coherent, more stable, and more equally accessible than any community is.

Kind of evidence. Qualitative and theoretical.

Open the Commitment 2 page

Commitment 3 · Activity register

Writing is a mode of learning and inquiry

What it means for teaching. Low-stakes and high-stakes writing help students formulate questions, represent and connect ideas, test claims, reflect, synthesize evidence, and revise their understanding. Writing belongs across the curriculum, in every discipline.

Its limit. Writing does not automatically produce learning.

Kind of evidence. Causal: a small positive average effect across 48 studies.

Open the Commitment 3 page

Tension to hold. Individual writer development and social and institutional conditions.

Reflection Prompt

Emig makes a theoretical claim. Bangert-Drowns, Hurley, and Wilkinson test it across 48 studies and find a small positive average effect. Before Thursday, predict what the meta-analysis found, then check. What does a claim look like when its theory is stronger than its measured effect?

The prompt guides the reflection in your Perusall annotation, and it opens Tuesday's discussion.
