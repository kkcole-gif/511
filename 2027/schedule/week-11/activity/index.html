In class · Thursday, November 4 · Week 11

Seminar discussion

No separate activity this Thursday. The room works the Reflection Prompt against Yancey, Robertson, and Taczak (2014), and returns to Bazerman et al. (2005) from Week 5.

Matched to Commitment 8: Writing development is lifelong and context-dependent and Commitment 9: Transfer is possible but not automatic.

Bring

  • Your Perusall annotations for Thursday's reading.
  • Your prediction, written before you read: what are Yancey, Robertson, and Taczak able to show with their design?
  • Your notes on Bazerman et al. from Week 5.

How the discussion runs

Three moves, in order. No clock.

  1. 1

    Move 1. Check the prediction

    Say what you predicted the study would show. Then say what its design let it show. Name the gap between the two.

  2. 2

    Move 2. Place the evidence

    Barnett and Ceci (2002) give a taxonomy, and a taxonomy reports no result. Name the kind of study Yancey, Robertson, and Taczak ran, and the kind of evidence each source offers.

  3. 3

    Move 3. Take the limit seriously

    Transfer should not be framed as a student deficit. Read one finding from the week as a claim about a course, and then as a claim about a student. What changes?

Reflection Prompt

Transfer is theoretically compelling and empirically thin. Barnett and Ceci come from psychology and give a taxonomy, not a result. Predict what Yancey, Robertson, and Taczak are able to show with their design, then check. What would it take to move this commitment's evidence?

You leave with one sentence on what it would take to move this commitment's evidence.
