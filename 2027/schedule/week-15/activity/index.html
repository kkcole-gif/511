In class · Thursday, December 2 · Week 15

Policy lab

You read one institutional AI policy as a claim and take it through four steps: its genre, its strain, its priorities, and the principle you would put in its place. Nothing is written up.

Matched to Commitment 12: Writing and communication are multimodal and technologically mediated.

Bring

  • A laptop, or a printed copy of the policy your group chooses.
  • Thursday's readings, annotated.

The session, step by step

Six steps in 75 minutes, 11:45 AM to 1:00 PM. Each block below is one step, sized by its minutes.

123456
  1. 1

    Step 1. Choose a policy

    5 min11:45 to 11:50

    In groups of three or four, choose one institutional AI policy: your own institution's, a department's, or one of the professional statements assigned this week.

  2. 2

    Step 2. Step one: genre

    10 min11:50 to 12:00

    Name the kind of document it is. Who issued it, and for whom? What does a document of this kind usually do?

  3. 3

    Step 3. Step two: strain

    15 min12:00 to 12:15

    Describe one real classroom situation the policy did not anticipate. Find the sentence where its language strains, and mark it.

  4. 4

    Step 4. Step three: priorities

    15 min12:15 to 12:30

    Read the strained sentence again. Whose priorities does the policy protect: students, instructors, the institution, or a vendor? Point to the words.

  5. 5

    Step 5. Step four: principle

    15 min12:30 to 12:45

    Write one governance principle to replace or extend the policy. State it in one sentence a committee would be able to adopt.

  6. 6

    Step 6. Report

    15 min12:45 to 1:00

    Each group reads its principle aloud. For each one, the room names the register it works in and the kind of evidence behind it.

The four steps come from the AI Policy Lab, one of Thursday's readings.

You leave with one principle in one sentence, and a position to carry into your Pedagogy Statement.
