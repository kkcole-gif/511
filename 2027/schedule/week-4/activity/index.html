In class · Thursday, September 16 · Week 4

Process map on paper

You draw how one piece of your own writing got written, in the order it happened. Then your group holds the maps against Flower and Hayes's model.

Matched to Commitment 4: Writing is recursive, developmental, and processual.

Suggested form. This week's Workshop Team designs and leads the session, and the team is free to adapt every step.

Bring

  • One piece of writing you finished in the last year. You need only remember how it went.
  • Paper and a pen. Colored pens help.

The session, step by step

Six steps in 75 minutes, 11:45 AM to 1:00 PM. Each block below is one step, sized by its minutes.

123456
  1. 1

    Step 1. Choose the piece

    5 min11:45 to 11:50

    Pick one piece you finished in the last year: a seminar paper, a syllabus, a cover letter, a grant narrative. Choose one whose writing you remember.

  2. 2

    Step 2. Draw the sequence

    15 min11:50 to 12:05

    Map what happened, in order, on one sheet. Draw the loops where you went back. Mark the stalls. Add the readers, the deadlines, the tools, and the places.

  3. 3

    Step 3. Compare maps

    15 min12:05 to 12:20

    In groups of three or four, lay the maps side by side. Where does every map loop? Where does one map differ from the rest?

  4. 4

    Step 4. Hold the maps against the model

    15 min12:20 to 12:35

    Put Flower and Hayes's model beside your maps. Find planning, translating, and reviewing on each map. Then list what your maps show and the model leaves out.

  5. 5

    Step 5. Report

    20 min12:35 to 12:55

    Each group reports one thing the model predicts and one thing it leaves out. Then test the limit of Commitment 4: does any map show a uniform sequence?

  6. 6

    Step 6. Close

    5 min12:55 to 1:00

    Write one sentence: if your map is accurate, what would a course need to give time for?

You leave with a map of your own process and a list of what a model of composing leaves out.
