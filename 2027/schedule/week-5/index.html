The commitments this week tests

Commitment 6 · Method register

Writing develops through use in consequential contexts

What it means for teaching. Students learn genres by using them in meaningful disciplinary, civic, professional, and public situations, not by memorizing templates. Disciplinary faculty hold essential expertise in how their fields make and communicate knowledge.

Its limit. Authenticity is not automatic, and simulated genres retain value.

Kind of evidence. Theoretical, historical, and qualitative.

Commitment 7 · Method register

Disciplines are ways of knowing, doing, and writing

What it means for teaching. Writing is constitutive of disciplinary activity: a lab report, a design brief, a case analysis, a proof, a policy memo, and an ethnography do different intellectual work. Carter (2007) calls writing a “metadoing,” a practice giving form to disciplinary ways of doing and knowing.

Its limit. The claim should not reify disciplines, which contain competing methods, genres, ideologies, and publics and which change over time.

Kind of evidence. Theoretical, historical, and qualitative.

Open the Commitment 7 page

Tension to hold. General writing knowledge and disciplinary specificity.

Reflection Prompt

No experiment settles whether writing is constitutive of a discipline. What kind of evidence do Carter, Miller, and Russell offer in its place, and why is it the right kind for a claim in the method register?

The prompt guides the reflection in your Perusall annotation, and it opens Tuesday's discussion.
