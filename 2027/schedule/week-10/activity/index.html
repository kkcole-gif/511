In class · Thursday, October 28 · Week 10

Citation trace on paper

You follow one claim backward through three sources and mark whose work receives credit at each step.

Matched to Commitment 10: Reading, research, writing, and citation are intertwined literacy practices.

Suggested form. This week's Workshop Team designs and leads the session, and the team is free to adapt every step.

Bring

  • A pen and a highlighter.
  • The team supplies the printed trace packet.

The session, step by step

Five steps in 75 minutes, 11:45 AM to 1:00 PM. Each block below is one step, sized by its minutes.

12345
  1. 1

    Step 1. Read the claim

    5 min11:45 to 11:50

    Each group receives a packet: one claim as a recent source states it, and the two sources standing behind it.

  2. 2

    Step 2. Trace it back

    20 min11:50 to 12:10

    Start with the most recent source. Underline the claim and circle its citation. Go to the cited source and find the passage. Note what changed on the way: the wording, the strength, or the scope. Repeat for the third source.

  3. 3

    Step 3. Mark the credit

    15 min12:10 to 12:25

    On the trace sheet, write three lists for each step: who is named in the sentence, who appears only in the citation, and whose work is present with no citation.

  4. 4

    Step 4. Post and compare

    25 min12:25 to 12:50

    Post the trace sheets and walk the room. Logan (2008) shows whose rhetorical education entered the record. Royster (1996) asks who speaks with authority about a community's knowledge. Where does each trace lose a name?

  5. 5

    Step 5. Turn to your own lists

    10 min12:50 to 1:00

    Open one bibliography of your own. Whose work does it credit? Write down what a count would show and what a close reading would show.

You leave with one traced claim and a first look at who your own citations credit.
