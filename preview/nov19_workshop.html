Opening

Two Example Policies

5 min

Read both examples. They take opposite positions. Both are written to be read by students on the first day.

Example AUse with disclosure

You may use AI tools to brainstorm, outline, or check citations. Each time you do, add a short note to your submission naming the tool, what you asked it, and what you kept. Drafting your sentences is your work. If a tool writes a passage, quote it and cite it like any other source.

Example BRefusal

This course does not use generative AI. We spend the semester on how your own thinking turns into language, and a tool would skip the part we are here to practice. If a disability or language access need makes this policy a barrier, come talk with me. We will find a path together.

Groups of three or four. Draft a policy for a first-year writing course, using Working Paper 2, McIntyre et al., and the values you wrote on Tuesday. Analog Draft on chart paper. Online Draft in a shared doc.

    Draft (18 min): Write one or two sentences per section in the voice a student reads.

Theoretical Lens Scenario

22 min
02
How would Freire, Inoue, and Baker-Bell respond?
ScenarioENG 101 · Week 6

Jordan, a multilingual student, submits a literacy narrative. The draft reads smoothly but sounds nothing like Jordan's in-class writing. In conference, Jordan explains running the draft through an AI tool to "make it sound academic," because a high school teacher had marked their English as wrong. The syllabus bans AI use.

  • 1
    Groups by theorist (12 min): Freire, Inoue, or Baker-Bell. Write a four- or five-sentence position statement in your theorist's terms. What is the problem here, and what should the teacher do next? Example opening for Inoue: "The problem starts with the standard Jordan was trying to meet."
  • 2
    Read aloud (10 min): Each group reads its statement. Where do the three agree? Does any of them change the policy your group drafted in Activity 01?
