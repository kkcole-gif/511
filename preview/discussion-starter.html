ENG 511 · Fall 2026 · Assignment

Discussion Starter

One session per student · approximately 20 minutes

Sign up by Week 1 ~20 min per session Tuesdays, Weeks 2–14
Approach
How You Open Is Up to You

You are not teaching for the day. You are a reader who has thought more carefully about this text than anyone else in the room, and you are inviting the class into a conversation about it. The goal is genuine engagement. Skip the presentation, the summary, and the performance of expertise.

These approaches work well
  • Draw the class's attention to a particular passage and spend time on it
  • Put ideas from different readings in conversation with each other
  • Discuss the rhetorical style of one or more of the texts
  • Show a video or connect the reading to a current cultural issue
  • Design an activity or short writing prompt inviting us into the texts
One rhythm tends to work: spend 2–3 minutes framing the reading by orienting us to what you found interesting, surprising, or worth arguing about. Then spend the remaining time in discussion. Follow threads as they open up.
Preparation
What to Bring

Come prepared with 3–4 questions you think would be useful for the class to explore. Two or three anchor questions will serve you better than a list of eight. You do not have to get through all of them.

Encouraged but not required

Develop a digital component for your discussion: a slide, a shared document, a visual, whatever fits your approach.

Bring a one-page handout for your classmates. I am happy to print copies or display it in class.

Purpose

The goals here are multiple: you gain experience describing complex ideas to your peers, you have the opportunity to explore the topics in composition you care about, and you practice synthesizing scholarship. Choose texts connected to your literacy narrative and applied pedagogy pathway.

You are also welcome to partner up. If you work with a colleague, coordinate beforehand and develop a shared strategy for opening discussion together.

On Flexibility

If you want to try something not listed above, check in with me beforehand. My default answer is yes. If something does not go the way you intended, we will work through it together. Discussion leading is a practice, not a test.

Read it carefully. Show up with something real to say or ask.
