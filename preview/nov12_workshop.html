Opening

Multimodality Before the Computer

5 min

Palmeri's title puts multimodality "before and beyond the computer." Banks pushes past modality as a category. Both widen what counts as composing. So today's tools include scissors and sticky notes along with Canva.

  • 1
    Full class (5 min): Name one analog multimodal composition from your own schooling. Example: a science fair trifold, a hand-drawn map for a history project, a mixtape with liner notes.

01

Multimodal Composing Demo

20 min
01
Make one small thing, then name what the tool did

In pairs, explain one course concept for a first-year student in a single artifact. Example concepts: "mutt genre," "retrofit," "audience stance." Pick one tool.

Analog
Paper, markers, sticky notes
Example: a one-page comic showing a student writing a mutt genre for no reader.
Visual
Canva or Adobe Express
Example: a three-slide Instagram carousel defining "retrofit" with one image per slide.
Audio or collaborative
VoiceThread or Padlet
Example: a 60-second voice explanation over one image, with space for classmates to reply.
  • 1
    Make (12 min): Build a rough version. Rough is the point.
  • 2
    Debrief (8 min): What did the tool make easy? What did it make impossible? Then the access check: would someone who is blind be able to use your artifact? Someone who is Deaf? Did the tool prompt you for alt text or captions, or leave you to remember?

02

Equitable Assessment of Multimodal Work

25 min
02
Assess the choices, account for access

Draft a short rubric for the artifact you made or the task you rewrote on Tuesday. A rubric for multimodal work often rewards polish, which rewards students who already own the software and the time. The example rows below show the difference. This work feeds your Portfolio Part 2 rubric revision.

CriterionRewards polish and prior accessRewards rhetorical choices
Design"Visuals look professional and polished.""The composer explains how the layout serves the intended audience."
Mode"Uses advanced editing features.""The chosen mode fits the purpose, and the account of choices says why."
Access(missing)"Images carry alt text, and audio or video comes with captions or a transcript."
Labor(missing)"The composer completed a draft, a peer review, and a revision."
  • 1
    Pairs (15 min): Write four criteria for your artifact or task. Include one on access and one on labor.
  • 2
    Swap and compare (10 min): Trade with another pair. Which of their criteria would a student with an old phone and no software budget meet as easily as a student with a new laptop?

03

Assignment Revision Lab

15 min
03
Add one multimodal component

Take the traditional assignment you brought. Add one multimodal component to open a form of knowledge the written version leaves out.

Example A literature review assignment keeps its written review and adds a hand-drawn or digital concept map showing how the sources talk to each other. The map shows relationships a linear review hides.
  • 1
    Solo (9 min): Write the new component as students would read it, in three or four sentences.
  • 2
    Pairs (6 min): What changed? What does the new component let a student know or show?
