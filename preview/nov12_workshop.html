In class · Thursday, November 12 · Week 13

Composing Before and Beyond the Screen

Ross opens on Palmeri. Then you all revise part of your Writing Assignment Design so students compose off the screen, and you draft a rubric that does not reward whoever owns the most supplies.

Readings: Palmeri, "Multimodality Before and Beyond the Computer" (2018) · Banks, "Beyond Modality" (2018)

Bring

  • Your Writing Assignment Design, the version you submitted October 25.
  • A laptop, or the assignment on paper.
  • The rubric you are revising for Portfolio Part 2, due Sunday, November 15.

On the table

  • Magazines, flyers, and campus print to cut from.
  • Scissors, glue sticks, and markers.
  • Blank paper, cardstock, sticky notes, and index cards.

The session, step by step

Six steps in 75 minutes, 11:45 AM to 1:00 PM. Each block below is one step, sized by its minutes.

123456
  1. 1

    Step 1. Discussion starter: Ross on Palmeri

    20 min11:45 to 12:05

    Ross leads on Palmeri, "Multimodality Before and Beyond the Computer." While Ross leads, write down one composing practice you would want students to try without a computer. You take it into Step 2.

  2. 2

    Step 2. Move one part off the screen

    17 min12:05 to 12:22

    In groups of three, take turns. Mark one part of your Writing Assignment Design and revise it so students compose in a mode off the screen. Keep a written component, and add a short account in which students explain their choices, the move Shipka builds into her framework. Write the revised task as students would read it, in four or five sentences. The materials on the table are there if you want to try the mode while you design it.

    Paper or objectExample: a hand-cut zine on a campus issue, made from printed sources students annotate in the margins.
    SoundExample: a two-minute recorded interview with a writer in the student's major, with a transcript.
    PerformanceExample: a three-minute in-class demonstration of how a genre works, followed by questions.
    Worked example: an ENG 101 rhetorical analysis, revised

    Before. Write a 1,200-word rhetorical analysis of an advertisement posted on campus. Identify the audience, purpose, and appeals.

    After. Choose an advertisement posted on campus. Build a counter-ad by hand from magazines, flyers, and other print you collect, aimed at an audience the original ignores. Then write a 600-word analysis of the original and a 300-word account of your choices: who your counter-ad addresses, what materials you used and why, and what the paper version let you do that a screen version would not.

  3. 3

    Step 3. Check access

    8 min12:22 to 12:30

    For each revised task, ask what a student would need to complete it: materials, money, time, space, a body able to cut, speak, or stand. Revise one line so a student without one of these still has a way in.

  4. 4

    Step 4. Draft four rubric criteria

    12 min12:30 to 12:42

    In pairs, write four criteria for one revised task from your group, including one on access and one on labor. A rubric for multimodal work often rewards polish, which rewards students who already own the materials, the software, and the time. The rows below show the difference. This work feeds your Portfolio Part 2 rubric revision.

    CriterionRewards polish and prior accessRewards rhetorical choices
    Design"The counter-ad looks professional and polished.""The composer explains how the layout serves the chosen audience."
    Mode"Uses a wide range of materials.""The chosen mode fits the purpose, and the account of choices says why."
    Access(missing)"The artifact comes with a description, transcript, or recording, so readers with different abilities reach its argument."
    Labor(missing)"The composer completed a draft, a peer review, and a revision."
  5. 5

    Step 5. Swap and compare

    8 min12:42 to 12:50

    Trade criteria with another pair. Which of their criteria would a student with no art supplies and a long commute meet as easily as a student with both time and materials at home?

  6. 6

    Step 6. Exit reflection

    10 min12:50 to 1:00

    Answer the prompt below on an index card and hand it in, or post it to Moodle before you leave the room.

Exit reflection

What happens when traditional assessment practices meet multimodal composition, and whose knowledge is valued?

You leave with one part of your assignment revised to work off the screen, and four rubric criteria for it, ready for your Portfolio Part 2 revision.
