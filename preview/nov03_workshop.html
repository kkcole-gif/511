Opening

Side Ramps and Front Doors

5 min

Dolmage describes the retrofit as "adding ramps at the sides of buildings and making accommodations to the standard curriculum." All three readings ask what happens when access arrives late, through a side entrance, one student at a time.

Dolmage, 2007
Mapping Composition: Inviting Disability in the Front Door
Dolmage maps composition as a space and asks who the field builds it for. Universal design, in his account, puts access into the building plan from the start.
Simpkins, 2018
Towards an Understanding of Accommodation Transfer
Simpkins interviews disabled students about how they secure accommodations course after course. "Accommodations are rhetorical," he writes, so students carry rhetorical skill from class to class to get them.
Tomlinson & Newman, 2017
Valuing Writers from a Neurodiversity Perspective
Tomlinson and Newman bring research on autism into composition pedagogy and ask teachers to read neurodivergent writers' choices as differences worth valuing.
  • 1
    Turn and talk (5 min): Name one side ramp you have seen in a course, as a student or a teacher. Example: a recording of the lecture shared only with students who had a letter, while everyone else took notes by hand.

Activity

Retrofit or Design?

30 min

This activity brings Simpkins in, the one reading no one led today. You all sort real course practices into two piles, then ask what each retrofit costs the student who needs it.

A
Eight practice cards, two piles

Analog Prof. Cole hands each group a set of printed cards. Sort them on the table into Retrofit (added later, for one student, on request) and Designed in (built in from the start, for everyone). Online Or drag the eight lines below into two columns in a shared doc.

CardCourse practice
AExtended time on in-class writing for students with a Disability Resource Office letter.
BEvery deadline carries a 48-hour grace window, no request needed.
CSlides shared after class with students who have a note-taking accommodation.
DSlides and a one-page outline posted before every class for everyone.
ECaptions added to course videos once a Deaf student enrolls.
FEvery video captioned and transcribed before the term begins.
GA separate quiet room for the one timed in-class essay, for students with documented need.
HStudents who apply through the DRO receive an alternative to the required oral presentation.
  • 1
    Sort (8 min): Groups of three or four. Notice which cards pair up. A and B answer the same need, as do C and D, and E and F. Two cards have no partner yet.
  • 2
    Simpkins lens (10 min): Simpkins writes, "Accommodations are rhetorical, meaning they depend on personal and institutional values and communication." For each card in your Retrofit pile, ask: What must a student write, say, or disclose to get this? To whom? How many times per semester, across how many courses?
  • 3
    Redesign (8 min): Write a designed-in partner for G and H. Example of the move, using card A: "Every in-class writing session ends with a 24-hour window to revise and resubmit online." The need stays met, and no one has to ask.
  • 4
    Report (4 min): Two groups read their partner for G or H. What does the designed-in version lose, if anything?
