Opening

Side Ramps and Front Doors

5 min

Dolmage describes the retrofit as "adding ramps at the sides of buildings and making accommodations to the standard curriculum." Both readings ask what happens when access arrives late, through a side entrance, one student at a time.

Dolmage, 2007
Mapping Composition: Inviting Disability in the Front Door
Dolmage maps composition as a space and asks who the field builds it for. Universal design, in his account, puts access into the building plan from the start.
Tomlinson & Newman, 2017
Valuing Writers from a Neurodiversity Perspective
Tomlinson and Newman bring research on autism into composition pedagogy and ask teachers to read neurodivergent writers' choices as differences worth valuing.
  • 1
    Turn and talk (5 min): Pairs. Name one moment, as a student or as a teacher, when access arrived through a side entrance: something you had to ask for, something that came late, or something you worked around alone. Example of a side ramp: a recording of the lecture shared only with students who had a letter, while everyone else took notes by hand. If no moment comes to mind, go back to the genre you named on your card last Tuesday and ask who in that course could produce it without asking for anything, and who had to ask.

Activity

Retrofit or Design?

30 min

Some course practices are added later for one student who asks. Others are built in from the start for everyone. Your group sorts eight of them, prices the ones students have to ask for, and designs two replacements.

A
What is on your table
  • 8
    Practice cards, A to H. Lay them face up in a row. They stay in the row.
  • 3
    Principle cards: Engagement, Representation, Action & Expression.
  • 2
    Blank cards, G′ and H′, for step 3.
  • 2
    Labels, Retrofit and Designed in, for reference.
  • 1
    Recorder sheet. Everything your group writes goes here, one line per answer.

Groups of three or four. Pick a recorder and a reader before you start. The definitions and the full list of cards are in the reference block below the steps.

B
What you do
  • 1
    Sort in pairs (8 min). The cards come in three pairs: A and B, C and D, E and F. Each pair meets one need in two ways.
    • The reader reads one pair aloud.
    • The group says which card is the retrofit and which is designed in.
    • For the designed-in card, the group picks the principle card it matches.
    • The recorder writes one line per pair: C retrofit. D designed in, Representation.
    G and H have no partner yet. Skip them until step 3.
  • 2
    Price the retrofits (10 min). Five cards are retrofits: A, C, E, G, H. For each one, answer three questions.
    • What must a student say or show to get this, and to whom?
    • How many times in a semester, across how many courses?
    • What happens to the student who never discloses?
    The recorder writes one line per card.
  • 3
    Design the missing pair (8 min). On card G′, write a designed-in version of G. On card H′, write a designed-in version of H. Under each, write the principle it uses.
    • Model, done for A: Every in-class writing session ends with a 24-hour window to revise and resubmit online. Same need as A, no request needed, principle Action & Expression.
  • 4
    Report (4 min). Two groups read one new card aloud and name its principle. The room answers one question: what does the designed-in version lose, if anything?
C
Reference

Retrofit: added later, for one student, on request. Dolmage: "adding ramps at the sides of buildings and making accommodations to the standard curriculum." Designed in: built into the course from the start, for everyone, with no request needed.

The three principle cards come from the CAST UDL Guidelines, version 3.0 (2024). Tomlinson and Newman recommend UDL for composition because the principles address "affect, engagement, and motivation as well as executive function, organization, attention, and working memory" (p. 105). Thursday goes into the guidelines beneath the principles. Today the names are enough.

UDL Principle 1
Engagement
The why of learning: interest, sustained effort, fewer threats and distractions.
UDL Principle 2
Representation
The what of learning: information in more than one form, such as text, audio, visual, or an outline.
UDL Principle 3
Action & Expression
The how of learning: more than one way to respond and show learning, with support for planning and pacing.

For step 2: Tomlinson and Newman note that "postsecondary students are not mandated to disclose diagnoses" (p. 91), so a retrofit reaches fewer students than need it. For step 3, their participants name what helps: varied assignment genres, specific criteria, tasks broken into manageable units, response consistent with those criteria, and ample time and scaffolding (p. 105).

CardCourse practice
AExtended time on in-class writing for students with a Disability Resource Office letter.
BEvery deadline carries a 48-hour grace window, no request needed.
CSlides shared after class with students who have a note-taking accommodation.
DSlides and a one-page outline posted before every class for everyone.
ECaptions added to course videos once a Deaf student enrolls.
FEvery video captioned and transcribed before the term begins.
GA separate quiet room for the one timed in-class essay, for students with documented need.
