Opening

Mode Inventory

6 min

You all composed in several modes before class today. Hocks gives us terms for reading digital texts as visual rhetoric. Shipka asks what happens when students choose the modes and materials of their work and explain those choices.

Hocks, 2003
Understanding Visual Rhetoric in Digital Writing Environments
Hocks offers three terms, "Audience Stance, Transparency, and Hybridity," to describe how visual rhetoric works in digital writing.
Shipka, 2005
A Multimodal Task-Based Framework for Composing
Shipka designs tasks in which students decide what to make, in what modes and materials, and then give an account of the goals and choices behind the work.
  • 1
    AnalogInventory (3 min): On an index card, list everything you composed since you woke up. Example: a text with an emoji, a voice memo, a grocery list, a calendar invite, a photo with a caption.
  • 2
    Count (3 min): How many modes are on your card? How many of them would count as "writing" in a first-year course?

Activity

Read It With Hocks, Redesign It With Shipka

30 min
A
Part 1: Read a page you know

The artifact is this course website. Open the ENG 511 course page and any workshop page from this semester. You have used both for weeks, so you all know their audience from the inside.

  • 1
    Pairs (12 min): Read the pages with Hocks' three terms. Audience stance: what does the page ask you to do, and who does it assume you are? Transparency: which design choices did you stop noticing after Week 2? Hybridity: where do text, color, and layout carry meaning together? Example to start: the color-coded Tuesday and Thursday cards tell you the kind of session before you read a word.
B
Part 2: Turn a prompt into a task

Take one traditional prompt and rewrite it Shipka's way. Students choose the mode, and they explain their choices.

Example ENG 101 · Before and after

Before: Write a 4-page essay analyzing a public space on campus.

After: Make something showing how a public space on campus shapes the people who use it. It might be a photo essay, a 3-minute audio walk, a hand-drawn annotated map, or a written essay. Along with it, write one page explaining what you set out to do, why you chose your mode and materials, and what you would change with more time.

  • 1
    Pairs (12 min): Rewrite a prompt from your own teaching, or use the researched-argument prompt from October 27. Keep the learning goal. Open the mode. Add the account of choices.
  • 2
    Share (6 min): Three pairs read their before and after. What does the account of choices let a teacher see?
