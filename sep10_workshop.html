01

Opening

3 min

Tuesday moved the problem of writing out of the writer and into the social. Today we build with the move. Three design activities, each ending in an artifact or a decision rather than a summary.

Giroux's frame for the day: Public pedagogy names the claim that education happens across institutions and media, not only in classrooms, and that intellectuals hold responsibility for the social relations their teaching produces. The question for each activity: what does it mean to design writing tasks and feedback practices as if this were true?
02

Designing Group Writing Tasks

25 min

Design a collaborative writing activity for an ENG 101 context, face-to-face or online, which enacts social construction theory. The question is structural: What structures support genuine co-authorship? What undermines it?

Every design names these five components. Your group should be able to say which one carries the theoretical weight.

The Product

What the group writes, for whom, and in what genre.

Labor

How work is divided, or deliberately not divided.

Disagreement

What happens when the group does not agree about a claim or a sentence.

Assessment

How the group's work is evaluated, and by whom.

The Individual

What each writer remains accountable for inside the collective.
Groups of 3–4

Design

  • 5 min. Choose a context: a specific course moment, a modality, a student population. Name the social claim your activity enacts, in one sentence.
  • 12 min. Draft the task on a shared document. All five components, written out. Mark the one doing the theoretical work.
Swap

Stress Test and Report

  • 5 min. Exchange designs with a neighboring group. Reader's task: find the moment where the design reverts to individual authorship with a social veneer. Return one sentence of critique.
  • 3 min. Two groups share the critique they received and what they would change.
Watch for: designs where collaboration means dividing a paper into sections. The theory requires knowledge made between people, not assembled from parts.
03

Anson on Error

20 min
Anson's claim: Error is not a property of texts. It is a judgment made by readers within social contexts. The same feature is an error in one setting and a resource in another. Response practice is therefore partly a matter of the reader's awareness of the context they read from. On Tuesday, Pratt's question about student error was the theoretical version. This is the practical one.
Sample Student Passage
ENG 101 · Literacy narrative, first draft · opening paragraph
Me and my brother learned to read at the same time even though he is two years older. My mom would sit us both at the kitchen table, she had this one book about a dog she read so many times the pages fell out. People say reading is something you do alone but in my house it was never like that, it was loud. When I got to middle school the teachers wanted us to read silent for 20 minutes and honestly it felt like punishment. I still read with my mouth moving a little which one of my professors pointed out like it was a problem. My grandma says in her village the old people read the newspaper out loud on the porch every morning so everybody heard the news, so maybe I got it from her. Anyways this is why I think reading should be taught as a group thing and not a test.

In pairs, two passes.

  • 1
    Pass one (4 min). Mark everything you would ordinarily flag. No discussion yet.
  • 2
    Pass two (8 min). Sort your marks into the three columns below. Then write one sentence of feedback for a single item in the middle column, addressed to the student, which names the mismatch without treating it as deficiency.
  • 3
    Share (4 min). Two or three feedback sentences read aloud. How would each be received by the student who wrote the passage? What does the sentence assume about who has to change?
Error by any standard
Features nearly every reader in nearly every context would read as a mistake.
Social mismatch
Register, dialect, genre convention, citation culture, audience assumption. Right somewhere, misread here.
Reader preference dressed as rule
Your habit, your training, your taste. Be honest about which marks belong here.
04

Contact Zone in Action

20 min

Identify a moment in a class you have taken or taught which felt like a contact zone. What happened? How might social construction theory have guided the response?

Pratt's literate arts of the contact zone Autoethnography · transculturation · critique · collaboration · bilingualism · mediation · parody · denunciation · imaginary dialogue · vernacular expression
Private, then trios

Describe and Reread

  • 5 min, alone. Describe the moment in five or six sentences. What clashed? Who held institutional authority? What was the outcome for the person with less of it?
  • 10 min, trios. Each person shares. Do not evaluate the response taken at the time. For each case, name one of Pratt's literate arts present in the room and unrecognized. Then propose one concrete change a teacher committed to social construction would have made.
Full class

Report

  • 5 min. One case per trio, two sentences: the unrecognized literate art and the change.
  • Pratt's concept requires asymmetry. A polite disagreement between peers of equal standing is not a contact zone. Where did the asymmetry sit in your case?
05

Exit Reflection

5 min
Write privately, as a Perusall post or in-class writing. You will not be asked to share unless you choose to. With Giroux in mind: the structures in question include the ones you benefit from.
Exit Reflection Prompt "What institutional structures support or hinder social approaches to writing in the courses you teach or will teach?"
