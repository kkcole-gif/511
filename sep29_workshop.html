Frame

The Three Registers

~7 min

This is the frame we've been building since Week 3. Today it goes to work on assessment. Use these three registers as analytical tools: when you read any of the week's texts, ask which register it's operating in — and which registers it's silent about.

Framework Writing as Activity · Pedagogy · Method
Register 1
Writing as Activity
What writers actually do: the cognitive, rhetorical, social, and material practices involved in producing text. Questions of process, revision, audience, constraint.
This week's silence None of this week's primary texts work primarily in Register 1 — but Thursday's activities do. What does that gap tell us?
Register 2
Writing as Pedagogy
How we teach: the values, assumptions, and instructional moves that structure a writing course. Questions of response, assignment design, what instruction is for.
This week Sommers (1982): comments should address future writing, not fix current text. Inoue: labor-based contracts change what pedagogy values.
Register 3
Writing as Method
Writing as a way of knowing: what counts as evidence, what gets measured, what research in the field can see. Questions of epistemology, not just practice.
This week Inoue: labor-based grading is a method argument — it changes what the course counts as evidence of learning. Smith: end comments as genre reveal the field's epistemological assumptions.

02

Reading Across the Registers

~28 min

Choose a register and a set of questions that's pulling at you. Form a group with others who chose the same one. After 12 minutes, we come back together and look for the places where the register arguments conflict.

How it works
  • 1
    Pick a register group below — the one where the question feels most unresolved for you. Three or four people per group.
  • 2
    In your group (12 min): Work through the questions. Where does your register argument get its force? Where does it go quiet? What would another register say about the same problem?
  • 3
    Return as a full class. Each group shares: what's the sharpest tension you found? We'll spend 10 minutes mapping where the three registers agree, disagree, and talk past each other.
Register 2 Pedagogy — Sommers & Inoue
Sommers found that teacher comments appropriated student texts — rewrote them in the teacher's voice rather than teaching revision. If you were designing a feedback protocol today, what assumption about ownership of the text would you need to make explicit?
Sommers is arguing for better pedagogy within a quality-based model. Inoue is arguing that the quality-based model is the problem. Is that a difference in degree, or a difference in register?
Inoue's labor-based contracts change what the course values pedagogically. But do they change what students do as writers — what happens in Register 1? Or do they leave the activity of writing untouched?
Register 3 Method — Inoue & Smith
Smith treats end comments as a genre. What does that analytical move reveal that treating them as "feedback" or "response" hides?
Inoue argues that "quality" is not a neutral descriptor — it encodes whose linguistic norms count as evidence of learning. Is that a pedagogical argument, a method argument, or both? What is the difference?
If end comments are a genre with predictable conventions, what does that predictability tell us about what teachers believe writing is? What does it tell us about what teachers think students need?
Cross-Register The Big Move
Sommers (1982) and Inoue (2019) are both responding to the failure of feedback. But they are not identifying the same failure. Map them onto the framework: what is each one claiming, and in which register?
The Feedback Portfolio you're building asks you to work in all three registers. Before Thursday, try to name: what would a Register 1 claim about feedback look like? A Register 2 claim? A Register 3 claim? Do any of the week's readings make all three?

03

Bridge to Thursday

~15 min

Thursday's workshop maps each register onto a different feedback practice — and asks you to evaluate each one from the others' vantage point. Come in having thought about where the three registers touch, and where they are genuinely incommensurable.

Take into Thursday All three of this week's texts claim to improve writing instruction. Do they agree on what "improvement" means? If Sommers, Inoue, and Smith sat down to design a feedback protocol together, where would they disagree — and in which register would the disagreement live?
