Assignment

Discussion Starter

One session per student · approximately 20 minutes

How You Open Is Up to You

You are not teaching for the day. You are a reader who has thought more carefully about this text than anyone else in the room, and you are inviting the class into a conversation about it. The goal is genuine engagement — not a presentation, not a summary, not a performance of expertise. Some approaches that work well:

  • Draw the class's attention to a particular passage and sit with it
  • Put ideas from different readings in conversation with each other
  • Discuss the rhetorical style of one or more of the texts
  • Show a video or connect the reading to a current cultural issue
  • Design an activity or short writing prompt that invites us into the texts

A rhythm that tends to work: spend 2–3 minutes framing the reading — not summarizing it, but orienting us to what you found interesting, surprising, or worth arguing about. Then spend the remaining time in actual discussion. Follow threads as they open up.

What to Prepare

Bring a set of 3–4 questions you think would be useful for the class to explore. Two or three anchor questions will carry you further than a list of eight — you do not have to get through all of them.

Encouraged but not required

Develop a digital component for your discussion — a slide, a shared document, a visual, whatever fits your approach.

Bring a one-page handout for your classmates. I am happy to print copies or display it in class.

Purpose

The goals here are multiple: you gain experience describing complex ideas to your peers; you have the opportunity to explore topics in composition that genuinely interest you; and you practice summarizing and synthesizing scholarship. This activity is meant to connect your literacy narrative to your applied pedagogy pathway — choose texts that matter to you.

You may also partner up. If you work with a colleague, coordinate beforehand and develop a shared strategy for opening discussion together.

The whole assignment

Read it carefully. Show up with something real to say or ask.

A Note on Flexibility

If you want to try something not listed above, check in with me beforehand — my default answer is yes. If something does not land the way you intended, we will move through it together. Discussion leading is a practice, not a test.
