Summative Feedback

~15 min
01
Register 3 · Method
Reading an end comment as a genre

Summative feedback evaluates a finished product — it makes a judgment. Smith's argument is that when we write end comments, we are performing a genre with its own conventions, purposes, and assumptions about what writing is. This activity asks you to read that genre as a method claim: what does the comment's structure reveal about what the teacher thinks writing is for?

  • 1
    Solo (3 min): Read the sample end comment from the ENG 511 Examples folder. Don't respond to it yet. Notice what it addresses, what it ignores, and what its structure implies about the purpose of writing.
  • 2
    Pairs (6 min): Apply Smith's genre analysis. What moves does the comment make? What does it assume the student needs? Is this comment for the grade, the revision, or the writer's future?
  • 3
    Report (6 min, full class): What did the genre analysis reveal that a pedagogical reading ("is this good feedback?") wouldn't have?
Register 3 analysis question

What does this comment assume "writing" is? Name what it measures, what it ignores, and what a student reading it would conclude about what their teacher thinks a paper is for.

02

Formative Feedback

~15 min
02
Register 1 · Activity Register 2 · Pedagogy
Responding to the writer, not the text

Sommers argued that effective feedback addresses the writer's future work, not just the current text's problems. Formative feedback intervenes in the writing process (Register 1) by naming what a writer is doing and what they might do next — it makes a claim about what writers do AND about what instruction should value.

  • 1
    Solo (4 min): Read the same student passage from the ENG 511 Examples folder without looking at the Activity 01 end comment. Write a one-paragraph formative response — addressed to the writer, not the grade — naming something they're doing well and opening one question for revision.
  • 2
    Trios (6 min): Read each other's responses. What does each one assume the writer needs to do? Does it "appropriate" the text — rewrite — or does it teach?
  • 3
    Report (5 min, full class): Where is Sommers' argument showing up? Where does formative feedback still default to summative judgment?
Register 1+2 analysis question

What does your formative response assume this writer is trying to do? Compare the assumptions in your response to the assumptions in the Activity 01 end comment.

03

Labor-Based Lens

~12 min
03
Register 3 · Method (repudiating)
What would we measure instead?

Inoue's argument is a method claim, not just a pedagogical preference. Labor-based grading says that measuring "quality" encodes whose linguistic norms count as evidence of learning. This activity applies that claim to what you built in Activities 01 and 02.

  • 1
    Pairs (6 min): Look at the end comment from Activity 01 and your formative response from Activity 02. What is each one measuring? What evidence of learning would a labor-based protocol count instead?
  • 2
    Full class (6 min): Synthesis question, below.
Synthesis question

Activities 01, 02, and 03 are not three opinions about feedback — they're three epistemological positions, three accounts of what counts as evidence that a student is writing. Which position can absorb the others' critiques? Which can't?

Feedback Portfolio — Draft Your Frame

~15 min

The last 15 minutes belong to your portfolio draft. Use today's activities as your working material: the end comment, your formative response, and the labor-based analysis are all candidates for your three feedback examples.

What the Feedback Portfolio asks

Select three pieces of feedback — given, received, or both — and analyze each one according to which register it operates in, what it assumes writing is, and what it makes visible or invisible. The framing essay (400–500 words) explains why the three examples together constitute a coherent claim about what feedback is for.

Before you leave
Three things to have before Sunday
  • Three feedback examples selected — note which register each one operates in and why you chose it.
  • One paragraph of analysis drafted for one example.
  • A working thesis for your framing essay — name what your three examples together reveal about what feedback is for.
