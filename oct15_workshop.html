01

Draft Workshop and Inclusion Audit

30 min
01
Whose voices does your assignment center? What bodies and identities does it assume?

You all audit each other's drafts with feminist and queer frameworks in hand. Groups of three, ten minutes per draft. Analog Trade printed drafts and write in the margins. Online Share your Google Doc with your group as a commenter.

  • 1
    Read (3 min): Everyone reads the first draft in silence, with the audit questions below beside it.
  • 2
    Audit (5 min): The two readers talk through the questions while the writer listens and takes notes. Writers, hold off on explaining. Your students will read the prompt without you in the room.
  • 3
    Writer's turn (2 min): The writer names one revision to make before Sunday, October 25. Then rotate to the next draft.
Audit questions
  1. The imagined student. Who does the prompt picture as its writer? List what it assumes about home, family, schedule, device, and body.
  2. Disclosure. Does the prompt ask students to share identity or personal history? If so, is there an equally valued path for a student who chooses not to?
  3. Gendered language. Check pronouns and examples against the NCTE Statement on Gender and Language (2018), which asks writers to avoid "binary alternatives such as he/she."
  4. Where thinking stops. Borrowing Winans' phrase, which part of the prompt treats one way of writing, knowing, or living as the default?
  5. Online access. OWI Principle 1 holds online writing instruction to being "universally inclusive and accessible." Does the task work on a phone, with a screen reader, and on an asynchronous schedule? See Hewett & DePew, Foundational Practices of OWI and the GSOLE OLI Principles.
  6. Hidden labor. Carrying Stenberg forward from Tuesday, what unpaid or invisible work does the task ask of students, and of you?

02

Bodies in Online Classrooms

13 min
02
What does embodied pedagogy look like on a discussion board?

Online courses often get described as places where bodies disappear. The prompt below, a common Week 1 post, says otherwise.

Sample prompt ENG 101 Online · Discussion Board · Week 1

Week 1 Introductions

Welcome, everyone! Post a short video (1 to 2 minutes) introducing yourself so we can put faces to names. Share your name, your pronouns, where you're from, and one fun fact about yourself. Then reply to at least three classmates by Sunday.

  • 1
    Pairs (6 min): What bodies does this prompt assume? Think camera, a quiet room, a face a student wants to show, a voice a student wants heard. What does it ask students to disclose? The NCTE statement asks us to respect a person's "chosen pronoun usage, or lack thereof." Does a required pronoun line honor that? Revise the prompt so every student has an equally valued way in.
  • 2
    Full class (7 min): Two pairs read their revisions. Then, how do Winans and Alexander & Wallace expand what embodied pedagogy means in online composition? Does anything from this revision belong in your own draft?
