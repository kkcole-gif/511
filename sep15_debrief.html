01

The Design

Read together

Everyone, the page you worked through today was built on the chapter you read for it. Nothing on it said so. The structure was Freire's, and the vocabulary was left off so you would meet the structure before the terms. This page puts the terms back.

The chapter's claim, in one line

Banking education deposits knowledge the teacher already holds into students who receive it. Problem-posing education puts a situation in front of teacher and students together, neither of whom holds the answer, and both of whom change by working on it. Today's page tried to be the second thing. Whether it was is the last question on this page.

Part by Part

The map

Each row names one thing the page did, the term Freire uses for it, and the version the page did not do. The second column is the version most session plans use, including most of mine.

StartThe teacher-student contradiction
What the page didThe plan was called a proposal. It named what I did not know. It gave you a vote at the end of Part 01 and a line on the screen where your decision was written. Freire's term for this is the resolution of the teacher-student contradiction: the teacher stops being the one who knows and the students the ones who do not, and both become, in his phrase, co-investigators. The authority did not disappear. It was stated, and made changeable.
What it did not doA schedule with times, activities, and outputs, authored in advance and delivered. This was the version of the page two hours before class. It was clearer. It was also a deposit.
Part 01Codification and decoding
What the page didThree of your own sentences, unattributed. The questions were what situation each one described and from where it was written. In Freire's culture circles the coordinator brought a codification, a picture or phrase representing the group's own situation, and the group decoded it by moving from the picture to the conditions producing it. Your sentences were the codifications. Nobody was waiting for a right answer, and the page said so.
What it did not doAsk you to sort the three sentences into the reading each one belongs to, with one of them being Freire's own claim. This was the earlier draft. It had an answer key.
Part 02Generative themes
What the page didAsked each of you to name a routine practice from your own first-year course before any list appeared. Freire's investigation begins from the learners' lives, from what he calls generative themes, because a problem worth posing is one the learners already inhabit. The six practices held in reserve at the bottom were the coordinator's prepared material, there in case the room's own themes did not surface.
What it did not doPut my list of six practices at the top and assign one to each group. Same activity, different origin. The practice would have been mine.
Part 03Problem-posing, and the teacher who learns
What the page didBuilt the scenario from whoever in the room had lived it. Left three empty slots for what each reading lets you say, and asked the class to fill them. I wrote a sentence too and it was heard among yours. Freire describes the teacher as one who is also taught, in dialogue with the students, and the empty slots were the concrete form of that: the page did not hold the readings' answers for you to receive.
What it did not doGive you the scenario I wrote and three answers I had already labeled Freire, Anzaldúa, and Wilson Logan. I had drafted those. They were deleted an hour before class. You would have been evaluating my readings of the texts instead of producing yours.
Part 04Praxis, and where knowledge goes
What the page didYour two sentences went onto the Padlet immediately, public to the group, as the first post in the section you will use Thursday. In banking, what students produce goes to the teacher to be checked. Here it went to each other, and it becomes the starting material for something you will make. Freire's word for reflection tied to action on the world is praxis. The page ended with a question rather than a statement about why the site you named matters, because the answer was yours to give.
What it did not doTell you to keep your two sentences in your notes for Thursday, and explain in a box why they matter. This was the earlier draft.
03

Where It Stayed Banking

Honest accounting

A page designed on Freire is still a page designed in advance by the teacher. The readings were assigned. The course is graded. You are here for a credential. Freire calls the conditions people cannot yet see past limit-situations, and the point of naming them is not to apologize for them but to make them visible enough to act on.

The limitation from Part 02 is the page's own limitation. One of you wrote: "Conforming is almost always easier than creating change." I asked what problem-posing means for people who came for the bank. The honest answer is that this page was one attempt at it inside an institution built for banking, and it worked exactly as far as you all decided to let it. The vote at the end of Part 01 was the test. What you chose, and whether the choice felt real, is the evidence.
The question for the room Where did today stay banking despite the design? Name one moment. Then name what would have had to change for it not to.

What This Means for Thursday

The bridge

On Thursday you all draft the opening of your microteaching lesson as a problem-posing prompt. You have now been inside one. The opening you write should do what Part 03 did: put a situation in front of students which comes from a site they already inhabit, and whose answer you do not hold. The site is the one you posted in Part 04. The test is the one from Part 01: is their answer able to surprise you?

Two things to carry out the door

Your section on the Padlet exists and has one post in it. The Thursday session page walks you through the rest, on your own time, in about the length of a normal Thursday.
