Opening

Three Readings, Three Decades

8 min

Flynn's title gives us today's question. If writing happens "as a woman," what does it mean to teach as a feminist? Flynn wrote in 1988, Richie and Boardman in 1999, and Stenberg in 2015. Each one places feminist work somewhere different.

Flynn, 1988
Composing as a Woman
Flynn reads student narratives alongside 1980s feminist research on gender and development, and asks whether women and men compose differently. Her focus is the writer and the text.
Richie & Boardman, 1999
Feminism in Composition
Richie and Boardman trace the stories composition tells about feminism's place in the field, and ask what each story includes and leaves out. Their focus is the discipline.
Stenberg, 2015
Repurposing Composition (excerpt)
Stenberg asks what feminist practice looks like inside a university run on efficiency and market logic, and proposes repurposing as a feminist response. Her focus is the institution.
  • 1
    AnalogQuick write (3 min): On an index card, finish this sentence: Teaching as a feminist means… A note on your laptop works too. Keep it. We come back to it at the end.
  • 2
    Read aloud (5 min): Three or four of you read your sentence to the room. No discussion yet. We are collecting starting points.

Activity

The Board: Where Does Feminist Work Happen?

28 min

Hadia and Kayla gave us Flynn and Stenberg. This activity brings in Richie and Boardman and puts all three side by side. You all build one shared board, one column per reading.

A
One board, three columns

Analog Draw the grid below on the whiteboard or on chart paper. Online If your group prefers laptops, one person opens a shared doc with the same grid and projects it for the gallery read.

RowFlynnRichie & BoardmanStenberg
Where does feminist work happen? (the writer, the classroom, the discipline, the institution)
What counts as evidence? Quote one line.
Who is the imagined student or teacher?
Who is missing? (race, class, sexuality, disability, language)
  • 1
    Pick a column (1 min): Form three groups, one per reading. The Richie and Boardman group gets the text no one has led yet, so it needs at least three people.
  • 2
    Fill your column (11 min): Work row by row. Quote the reading in row two. Its own words carry the argument better than a paraphrase.
  • 3
    Gallery read (4 min): Everyone reads the whole board in silence. Mark one cell where two columns disagree.
  • 4
    Full class (12 min): Start with the disagreements you marked. Then take the bottom row. Which reading accounts best for race, class, sexuality, and other identities? Where do all three go quiet? Last, Stenberg's institutional argument: what structures in our own program would repurposing touch?
