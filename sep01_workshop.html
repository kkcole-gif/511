Phase 1: Expert Groups
12 min

Divide into your reading groups. Use these 12 minutes to consolidate what you know and prepare to teach it. Leave this phase with: a one-sentence claim, one teaching implication, and one genuine question the text raised for your group.

Perl · 1980
"Understanding Composing"
  • 1
    What does Perl mean by felt sense? How is it different from a thesis or an outline?
  • 2
    Where does her observation of student writers challenge what teachers typically see?
  • 3
    What does a teacher need to stop doing if Perl is right?
Leave with: one claim, one implication for feedback, one open question.
Hartwell · 1985
"Grammar, Grammars, and the Teaching of Grammar"
  • 1
    Map the five grammars quickly. Which ones operate in the writer's head? Which ones operate in the classroom?
  • 2
    Which grammar do most writing teachers deploy in instruction? Which do student writers actually use when they compose?
  • 3
    What does Hartwell's argument mean for how we respond to "error"?
Leave with: one claim, one implication for error response, one open question.
Flower & Hayes · 1981
"A Cognitive Process Theory of Composing"
  • 1
    What are the three main components of the cognitive process model? How do they interact?
  • 2
    What does "task environment" mean, and why does it matter that they included it?
  • 3
    How does the model change what we're looking for when we observe a student writer?
Leave with: one claim, one observational implication, one open question.

Phase 2: Mixed Teaching Groups
18 min

Regroup into trios — one Perl reader, one Hartwell reader, one Flower & Hayes reader.

  • Each teacher — ~4 min
    State your article's central claim in one sentence. Offer one implication for teaching or feedback. Pose your group's open question.
  • Synthesis — 6 min
    Work through one of the prompts below together, or follow wherever the teaching led you.
Synthesis prompts
A
What do all three texts assume about the writer as an individual? Where do those assumptions come from?
B
Perl cares about felt sense. Flower & Hayes care about cognitive load. Hartwell cares about grammatical knowledge that already exists. How do these concerns fit together — or do they?
C
Which of these frameworks would be most generative for the way you currently think about writing instruction? Most challenging?

Literacy Narrative — Project Discussion
15 min
📝
What does "multimodal" mean for your specific literacy history? Does your story have a visual or sonic dimension that a written essay couldn't carry?
🔍
What's the difference between describing your experiences as a writer and analyzing them? Perl, Flower & Hayes, and Hartwell all observe writers from the outside — what would it mean to turn that lens inward?
✏️
The teaching philosophy draft is described as "initial" intentionally. What does it mean to write something you know will change? What do you commit to now versus hold loosely?
